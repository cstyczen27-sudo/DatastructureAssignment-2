In my linked list, it works by first asking the user where they want to put the object: 
at the start of the list or at the end. Then, once they have done that, they input 
what the object should be called, input the name, and it creates a Node out of the 
object that was made, putting it either at the start of the list or at the end of the 
list depending on the user's input.


The role that the head plays in my list is that it acts as the starting point for 
when you need to search within the linked list. For example, in my remove method, 
it starts at the head of the list, moves through the list until it finds the Node 
it is looking for, severs the Node from the previous Node, and joins it with the 
Node right after the removed Node. 

An engineer might need this type of list for something like a waiting line. 
The example of the assignment is doing another real-world example would be 
like a digital waiting line for telling you when your food is ready. 
When your food is being made, you are in one list; when your food is ready, 
you are put into another, and when you pick it up or it is delivered to you, 
you are taken off all linked lists.
